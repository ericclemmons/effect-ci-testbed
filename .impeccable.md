## Design Context

### Users
Developers and release operators reviewing CI and Cloudflare rollouts in Slack or
GitHub, including emergencies. They need to understand previous/new metrics,
release progress, and available actions without opening a custom approval screen.

### Brand Personality
Direct, compact, trustworthy. Communicate measured evidence honestly; uncertainty
and explicit risk acceptance must never become a fabricated healthy result.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use native Slack/GitHub presentation, compact linked repository/short-SHA/details
context, meaningful status icons, and one updated message per intentional run.
Avoid redundant labels, unfamiliar internal terminology, default comment modals,
and notifications for ordinary CI. Preserve Slack's native theme and typography.

### Design Principles
- Show the previous metric and new metric together, with counts and uncertainty.
- Keep Progress/Reject direct and current-stage-specific; comments are opt-in.
- After rollback, show what failed and the successful restoration, not skipped stages.
- Keep the external Dashboard and Preview links together in the parent context.
  The first thread reply is a maintained Status/Step table linking to each step's
  evidence reply, not a repeated Field/Value metadata table.
- Never trade authentication, evidence integrity, or recovery for presentation.
- Keep the workflow title, rotating current step/status and latest evidence in
  the parent card. Preserve the latest chart after completion. Non-broadcast
  thread replies contain only their step and its evidence/output/controls—no
  numbering or repeated workflow/repository/details headers.
- Put a plain status glyph first (►, ✓, ✗, ∗). Preserve the final completed step
  in the title. Show step-count progress, never a time/health estimate; use a native
  table for the first step-index reply.
